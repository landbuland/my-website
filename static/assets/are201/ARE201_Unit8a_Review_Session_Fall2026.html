Production & Costs: Review

Pine Hollow Farm, one digger, more and more workers

Part A · Diminishing returns · LO 8a.1 · recap

Diminishing returns in one slide

With technology and other inputs held fixed, each added unit of one input eventually adds less to total output.

Where it begins

At the first MP smaller than the one before.

Pine Hollow: MP = 40, 60, 50, … Kenji, the 3rd worker.

Why

A fixed input: every worker shares the same digger and the same rows.

Not lazier workers. A short-run story.

Trap

MP falling ≠ output falling.

TP keeps rising while MP > 0. Only a negative MP makes TP fall.

Typical shape: MP rises, then falls (Pine Hollow). The law itself: MP eventually falls—it can fall from the very first unit, as with fertilizer.
Part A · Diminishing returns · LO 8a.1 · Q1

Does total output fall?

“The principle of diminishing returns implies that total production decreases when more units of a variable input are added.”

Answer: False.

Diminishing returns means each extra unit adds less output, not that total output falls. TP keeps rising as long as MP > 0.

Part A · Diminishing returns · LO 8a.1 · Q2

Why do diminishing returns happen?

Which of the following is the primary reason for experiencing diminishing returns to labor?

Answer: C · fixed inputs.

Workers share a fixed input (land, a machine), so each extra worker eventually adds less. Demand has nothing to do with the production function.

Part A · Diminishing returns · LO 8a.1 · Q3

Hiring more laborers on fixed acres

You have a fixed amount of acres to harvest. As you hire more laborers, what does the law of diminishing returns predict?

Answer: D · the best option offered.

The key point is that MP falls. “Rises, then falls” is the typical shape; the law only requires that MP eventually falls. C is wrong: MP turns negative only when total output starts to fall.

Part A · Diminishing returns · LO 8a.1 · Q4

Which bagel shop shows diminishing returns?

Which of the following is true about the law of diminishing returns? A small bagel shop hires more people for its busiest shift.

Answer: A.

MP = 10, 6, 2, then negative: each hire adds less. B shows rising MP; C shows constant MP.

Part A · Diminishing returns · LO 8a.1 · Q5

Too much pesticide

A farmer sprays pesticides to repel insects. The first spraying keeps insects from harming the crop. As the farmer sprays more and more, he eventually kills the crop. What economic principle does this best represent?

Answer: A.

Each extra spraying adds less protection; eventually its marginal product is negative and output falls.

Part A · Diminishing returns · LO 8a.1 · Q6

No diminishing returns at all

A production function always doubles output when the input doubles, with no diminishing returns to any input. With output on the vertical axis and input on the horizontal axis, what does it look like?

Answer: C · a straight line through the origin.

Constant MP means every unit adds the same amount, so TP rises in a straight line. B is what diminishing returns look like.

Part C · Fixed, variable and average cost · LO 8a.3 · recap

Every cost formula on one page

TC = FC + VCTotal cost is the fixed bill plus the variable bill.
FC = TC at Q = 0Quiz tables hide FC in the first row.
AFC = FC ÷ QDouble Q, halve AFC. Always falls.
AVC = VC ÷ QVC = TC − FC first, then divide.
ATC = TC ÷ Q = AFC + AVCThe full cost of a typical unit.
MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQWith ΔQ = 1, just the jump in TC. Also = wage ÷ MP.
Working backward: VC = AVC × Q, TC = ATC × Q, FC = AFC × Q. Fixed or variable? Ask: does this cost change if we produce more this period?

Part C · Fixed, variable and average cost · LO 8a.3 · Q16

Working backward from AVC

Terry plants corn. She produces 100 bushels, her average variable cost is $5 per bushel, and total fixed costs are $100.

(a) What is her variable cost?

VC = AVC × Q = $5 × 100 = $500.

(b) What is her total cost?

TC = FC + VC = $100 + $500 = $600.

(c) What is her average total cost?

ATC = $600 ÷ 100 = $6. Check: AFC $1 + AVC $5.

Part C · Fixed, variable and average cost · LO 8a.3 · Q17 · (a) Exam 2 F24

Spreading the same fixed cost

In both parts, total fixed cost stays the same.

(a) The AFC of producing 600 eggs is $12. What is the AFC of producing 1,200 eggs?

FC = $12 × 600 = $7,200; $7,200 ÷ 1,200 = $6. Double the output, halve the AFC.

(b) A chocolate company’s AFC is $3 per bar at 100 bars. What is AFC at 600 bars?

FC = $3 × 100 = $300; $300 ÷ 600 = $0.50. Six times the output, one-sixth the AFC.

Part D · Reading cost curves · LO 8a.3 · Q18

Read the cost curves

The graph shows cost curves for a typical firm. Choose from MC, ATC, AFC, AVC.

(a) Which curve is Curve 1 (red)? Curve 2 (blue)?

Curve 1 = MC, Curve 2 = AVC (Curve 3 = ATC). MC is the steep curve through the bottoms of both U’s; AVC is the lower U.

(b) Average variable cost at 2 units? At 3 units?

Read Curve 2: $7 at Q = 2, $8 at Q = 3.

(c) At what price does MC intersect AVC?

$7, at AVC’s minimum (Q = 2).

(d) True or false: the gap between ATC and AVC is always larger at lower quantities.

True. The gap is AFC = FC ÷ Q, which shrinks as Q grows.

Part D · Reading cost curves · LO 8a.3 · Q19

Name the curves: M, N and O

Curves M, N and O are a firm’s cost curves.

(a) Which curve represents marginal cost?

M: the steep curve that passes through the lowest points of N and O.

(b) Which curve represents average variable cost?

O: the lower U. N is ATC; the gap between them is AFC, and it narrows as Q grows.

Tip: MC crosses AVC first (at a smaller Q), then ATC.

Part D · Reading cost curves · LO 8a.3 · Q20

If fixed costs were zero

If fixed costs equal 0, what is true about the ATC and AVC curves?

Answer: A.

ATC = AFC + AVC. With FC = 0, AFC = 0 at every output, so ATC and AVC coincide.

Part D · Reading cost curves · LO 8a.3 · Q21

Can AFC be above ATC?

“The average fixed cost curve can never be above the average total cost curve.”

Answer: True.

ATC = AFC + AVC, and AVC is never negative, so ATC ≥ AFC everywhere.

Part E · MC and ATC · LO 8a.4 · recap

The marginal unit pulls the average

MC = ATC

ATC is at its minimum.

MC crosses ATC (and AVC) at the bottom of the U.

ATC rises.

Quiz average 85, next quiz 90 → average 86.

New ATC = (old TC + MC) ÷ new Q. Example: 10 units at ATC $20, the 11th has MC $9 → ($200 + $9) ÷ 11 = $19.
Trap: “MC is rising, so ATC must be rising.” Compare the levels, not the direction: at Pine Hollow MC rises from $2 to $6 while ATC keeps falling.
Part E · MC and ATC · LO 8a.4 · Q22

A fifth toy

You produce toys. Currently you produce 4 toys at a total cost of $40.

(a) What is your average total cost?

$40 ÷ 4 = $10.

(b) You produce a fifth toy, and its marginal cost is $5. What is ATC now?

($40 + $5) ÷ 5 = $9.

(c) Why did ATC change?A. The MC of the fifth toy was lower than the previous ATC · B. The price of materials fell

A. A marginal unit below the average pulls the average down. Nothing happened to input prices.

Part E · MC and ATC · LO 8a.4 · Q23

Which statements are correct?

Select all of the following statements that are correct.

Answer: C and D.

A: MC counts only costs that change with one more unit; FC does not. B has the direction backward. D: MC = wage ÷ MP, so a falling MP means a rising MC.

Part E · MC and ATC · LO 8a.4 · Q24

Where MC crosses the averages

“The marginal cost curve always intersects the ATC and AVC curves at their minimum points.”

Answer: True.

Left of the crossing MC < average, so the average falls; right of it MC > average, so the average rises. The crossing is the bottom. Same for AVC.

Cheaper flour at Jenny’s bakery

The price of flour, Jenny’s primary input, has fallen because of a surplus in wheat production. All else equal, how will this affect her average total cost (ATC) curve?

Part F · What moves the curves · LO 8a.3 & 8a.5 · Q31

No growth hormones

Organic dairy farms may not use synthetic growth hormones. How does this most directly affect the marginal cost (MC) of producing milk?

Answer: B.

Less milk per cow is lower productivity: more inputs per gallon, so MC rises (the third row of the recap table).

Part F · What moves the curves · LO 8a.3 & 8a.5 · Q32 · Exam 2 F24

Cage-free eggs in the short run

Demand for cage-free eggs increases significantly, and farmers increase their production in the short run. What is likely to happen to the marginal cost of producing those eggs?

Answer: B.

In the short run the barns are fixed, so producing more runs into diminishing returns and MC rises. Economies of scale are a long-run idea.

Part F · What moves the curves · LO 8a.3 & 8a.5 · Q33

Organic vs. nonorganic production functions

How would you compare the production function of an organic farm to that of a nonorganic farm?

Answer: A.

With the same inputs, organic rules (no synthetic fertilizer or hormones) give less output, so the organic production function lies below. Same land does not mean same output.

Part F · What moves the curves · LO 8a.3 & 8a.5 · Q34 · Final Exam

Back to supply: fertilizer costs more

Fertilizer, an input for corn production, becomes more expensive. Due to higher fertilizer prices, ____ for corn ____.

Answer: A · supply decreases.

A higher input price raises MC, so the supply curve shifts left. “Quantity supplied” would be a movement along the curve, caused only by a change in the corn price.
