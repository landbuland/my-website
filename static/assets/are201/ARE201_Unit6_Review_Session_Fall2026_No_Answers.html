Six tools cover every question today

1 · Midpoint method

%ΔQ = ΔQ ÷ average Q

%ΔP = ΔP ÷ average P

|Ed| = %ΔQ ÷ %ΔP

2 · Compare to 1

> 1 elastic · < 1 inelastic

= 1 unit elastic

0 perfectly inelastic (vertical)

∞ perfectly elastic (horizontal)

3 · Total revenue

Elastic: price ↑ → TR ↓

Inelastic: price ↑ → TR ↑

Unit elastic: TR unchanged

4 · Supply

Es = %ΔQs ÷ %ΔP

Always positive—no absolute value needed.

More elastic the more time sellers have.

5 · Read the sign

Eincome > 0 normal · < 0 inferior

Ecross > 0 substitutes · < 0 complements

6 · Taxes

The more inelastic side of the market bears more of the tax—no matter who writes the check.

Class convention: report |Ed|—write 0.45, not −0.45. Published tables often keep the minus sign: read the size first, then the sign.

Before you leave

Six habits prevent most elasticity mistakes

Use the midpoint, every time

Average P and average Q are the bases, so the answer is the same in both directions.

MEASURE

Compare |E| to 1

That one comparison gives both the label and the total-revenue rule.

CLASSIFY

Say the number as a sentence

|Ed| = 0.7: a 10% price rise cuts quantity demanded 7%. Inelastic is not zero.

INTERPRET

Read the sign

Income: + normal, − inferior. Cross: + substitutes, − complements. Supply: always +.

SIGNS

Find the trapped side

The more inelastic side bears more of a tax, no matter who writes the check.

TAXES

Supply shocks meet inelastic food demand

Price moves more than quantity, so farm revenue moves against the size of the harvest.
