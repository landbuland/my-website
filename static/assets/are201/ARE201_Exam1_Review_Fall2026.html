Transfer

A different price redistributes the gains on units that still trade.

One side’s gain is the other’s loss.

Lost trade

A buyer values a unit more than it costs, but the transaction no longer occurs.

The potential total gain disappears.

“All producers benefit” is too strong.
A higher floor helps some sellers who sell, but excludes others.

A ceiling helps some buyers but excludes others. Lines or lotteries may ration access.

Areas assume the highest-WTP buyers and lowest-cost sellers trade. Other rationing can reduce surplus further.

Final check · choose, calculate, explain

Can you explain every step?

1 · Trade: Farm C can produce 40 apples or 20 pears; D can produce 30 apples or 10 pears. Who has comparative advantage in pears?

C: 1 pear costs 2 apples, versus 3 for D. Compare opportunity costs, not just output.

2 · Markets: Cheaper pasta reduces demand for substitute rice. At the same time, higher fuel costs reduce rice supply. What happens to P and Q?

D left and S left: Q falls; P is ambiguous. The quantity effects agree, but the price effects oppose.

3 · Price floor: Qd = 36 − 2P and Qs = P. A floor sets P = $15. How many units trade, and how much excess supply is there?

P* = $12. At the binding floor, Qd = 6 and Qs = 15: 6 units trade; excess supply = 9.
