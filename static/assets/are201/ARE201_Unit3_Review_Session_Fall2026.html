Key concept 1

Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF)

A PPF shows the maximum feasible combinations of two outputs that can be produced with current resources and technology.

The trade-off: moving along the frontier means producing more of one output and less of the other.

Remember: a PPF describes production possibilities—not preferences and not what a person should choose.

On = efficient Inside = inefficient Outside = unattainable
Key concept 2

Absolute Advantage

A producer has an absolute advantage if the producer can make more output with the same quantity of inputs.

Fair comparison: hold the input fixed—such as one hour of work—then compare output rates.

Different total time endowments can produce misleading endpoint comparisons. Compare productivity using the same amount of time.

How to identify it

1

Choose one output

Compare producers one good at a time.

2

Use the same input

Convert both technologies to output per hour.

3

Find the higher rate

More output per hour means absolute advantage.

productivity = output ÷ input
Key concept 3

Comparative Advantage

A producer has a comparative advantage in an output when the producer has the lower opportunity cost of producing it.

Specialization rule: assign each task to the producer who gives up less of the other output.

A producer can be better at everything in absolute terms but still lack comparative advantage in one activity.

Keep the units visible

OC of 1 unit of X = Y forgone ÷ X gained
1

Calculate both costs

Find each producer's opportunity cost of X.

2

Compare like with like

Use the same units for both producers.

3

Choose the lower cost

Lower opportunity cost means comparative advantage.

Today's interactive practice

Siri & Alexa: Cookies and Windows

The background

Siri and Alexa make cookies for neighborhood friends. Before going to the park, they must wash all 45 windows in their house.

Each sibling has 3 hours (180 minutes). Time spent washing windows cannot be used to make cookies.

Team goal: wash all 45 windows and make as many cookies as possible.

Production technology

Siri

6 windows
in 15 minutes

15 cookies
in 30 minutes

Alexa

5 windows
in 10 minutes

40 cookies
in 60 minutes

You will be assigned one role and work with a partner through four instructor-paced rounds. Stay in one browser tab and wait at each stage gate.
1

Draw and Check a PPF

Work from the productivity numbers for your assigned role.

PPF
1

Find your two intercepts

Use the full 180 minutes to calculate all-cookies output and all-windows output.

2

Draw your PPF

Choose one endpoint on each axis; the activity connects them with a straight line.

3

Check your partner

Inspect your partner's endpoints and mark the PPF correct or incorrect.

What to pay attention to

  • Horizontal axis: cookies.
  • Vertical axis: windows.
  • Use all 3 hours at each intercept.
  • Do your own calculation before judging your partner.
Round complete when: both partners submit a PPF, check the other PPF, and review the correct line.
2

Calculate Opportunity Costs

Opportunity cost must include the output being given up.

OC
1

Calculate your own costs

Find the windows forgone per cookie and the cookies forgone per window.

2

Keep the units attached

Decimals are allowed; round to two decimal places when needed.

3

Check your partner's numbers

Judge each of the two submitted opportunity costs separately.

Two calculations

OC of 1 cookie
= windows forgone ÷ cookies gained
OC of 1 window
= cookies forgone ÷ windows gained
Round complete when: both partners submit both costs, check both of the other person's numbers, and read the result screen.
3

Judge Two Claims

Use opportunity cost—not speed alone—to defend your position.

Claims
1

Respond to Claim 1

Agree or disagree, then give at least one full sentence explaining why.

2

Respond to Claim 2

Again choose a position and justify it from your assigned role's point of view.

3

Compare your reasoning

Read your partner's responses and prepare to negotiate the window split.

The two claims

“The siblings should each wash the same number of windows.”
“Alexa should wash all 45 windows while Siri makes cookies.”
Before moving on: compare the two opportunity costs of washing one window. Ask which sibling gives up fewer cookies per window.
4

Negotiate and Calculate

Reach one binding contract that assigns all 45 windows.

Contract
1

Propose a whole-number split

Enter windows washed by Siri from 0 to 45; Alexa washes the remainder.

2

Review output before accepting

Use the preview to compare Siri's cookies, Alexa's cookies, and the team total.

3

Accept and verify the contract

After agreement, independently calculate the total cookies produced.

Contract rules

  • Either partner may propose a split.
  • Only the other partner may accept the current proposal.
  • An accepted proposal is final for this activity.
  • For each sibling: subtract washing time from 180 minutes, then use the remaining time for cookies.
Final goal: satisfy the 45-window requirement and maximize the pair's total cookie output.
